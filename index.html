About

From the newsroom to the geodatabase.

I didn't start in code. I bring a decade of broadcast journalism experience with renowned TV channels in Bangladesh, most recently as an environmental, business & economic journalist for ATN Bangla TV in Dhaka — interviewing climate scientists and policymakers, and realizing that the stories I was telling about land, forests and cities needed better data behind them than most newsrooms had access to.

That pulled me into a Master's in Ecosystem Services at TU Dresden, and then deep into the technical end of environmental science: Python, PostGIS, and the kind of geospatial engineering that turns satellite imagery and LiDAR point clouds into answers. Today I'm finishing a second Master's in Information Technology at HNEE, writing the scripts that process raster and vector geodata for forestry, biodiversity and urban-climate research.

Programming started for me as a research tool and became the part of the work I like best. From Python scripts I moved into full-stack web development with TypeScript, React, Next.js and Node.js, and I now build and deploy complete web apps such as HandsTogether, Market Price and FitLog.

What I enjoy most is the middle ground: taking a question a policymaker or ecologist actually cares about, and building the pipeline — in Python, QGIS, or a Leaflet map — that answers it with real numbers instead of guesses. I'm equally comfortable automating a messy geodatabase or presenting findings at an international conference.

Outside of work I'm usually still outdoors — hiking the forests around Eberswalde with a camera, following football, or tinkering with whatever new satellite dataset Google Earth Engine has just made public. I also keep a working B1 in German and native Bangla alongside fluent English, which has come in handy while studying and researching across six countries: Bangladesh, Germany, Poland, Spain, Austria and Italy.
